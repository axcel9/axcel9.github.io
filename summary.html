Technical Synthesis

Within those nine modules, I have understood that technology works best when hardware, software, security and communication work together. The hardware module gave me the basis for understanding that each computer relies upon components such as the CPU, RAM, Storage Device, and GPU to function properly. This connects to the Upgrade or Replace module since an Information Technology (IT) professional would not be able to determine whether a computer meets the needs of current generation software until he/she examines the current specifications of the system. Programs such as Adobe Creative Cloud, Microsoft Teams, Microsoft Excel and Microsoft PowerPoint all require enough memory, storage space and processing power to operate without issue. The software modules demonstrated to me how computers are utilized to organize and transmit information. Through working with Microsoft Excel, I realized that formulas, totals, percentages and charts can transform the sales data into a means of making effective business decisions. By utilizing Microsoft PowerPoint, I realized that slides, themes, transitions and animations can enable an audience to better comprehend information. Both of the above mentioned modules connect to the writing and paraphrasing module because technology does not merely involve utilizing programs; it also involves providing clear explanations regarding information, responsibly citing sources, and preventing plagiarism. The troubleshooting, networking and privacy modules demonstrated to me how crucial it is to think similarly to an IT support professional.

While completing the AI troubleshooting module, I recognized that AI could assist in resolving technical difficulties, however the user must provide a clear question and still verify the response received. The Wi-Fi module related networking to the everyday world by demonstrating that bandwidth represents the speed paid for by the customer, whereas throughput represents the actual speed received. Distance between devices, walls, router placement, and security settings can affect network performance and therefore have relevance for school, work, video calls, and online services. Both the privacy and programming modules relate to the remaining portions of the course. The privacy module illustrated that applications, websites, and previous account activity may collect or retain personal information; thus, customers must control access permissions and safeguard their digital footprint. The programming module illustrates how software reacts to user input; therefore, the logic underlying many applications, websites, games, and tools resides within user input. All the above modules relate to one another because real IT work requires knowledge of machines, proficiency with software application usage, problem-solving capabilities, protection of customers, and clarity in communication.
